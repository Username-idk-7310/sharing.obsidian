# Bias

**Participant bias**  
- Participant (sub)consciously influences outcome of study thru participants' expectations, motivations, or behavior

- Expectancy effect

- Social desirability effect

- Screw-you effect

- Reactivity

- Placebo effects

- demand characteristics

  

==**Researcher bias**==  
- Researcher (sub)consciously influences data/finding with their belief/expectations.

- Confirmation bias

- Interviewer bias

  

- ==**Controlling researcher bias in quantitative research**==
    
    1. **Random allocation** of participants to experimental/control groups ensures that group differences occur by chance rather than researcher influence.
    
    1. **Anonymize participant data** during analysis  
        ~ prevents researchers from associating individual participants with specific outcomes.
    
    1. **Standardized procedure**
        
        ~ All researchers involved in the study must be trained in the procedure.  
        ~ minimizes subjectivity/ influences by personal biases.
        
    
    1. **researcher triangulation**  
        ~ Multiple analysts (e.g. researchers/statisticians) not connected to the research verify the findings independently
    
    1. **Method and data triangulation**  
        ~ cross-check results, reducing the risk of bias tied to a single approach.
    
    1. **peer review + replication**  
        ~ identify and address potential sources of bias + confirm the findings.
    

- ==**Controls for researcher bias in qualitative research**==
    
    1. **Peer review** 
        
        ~ one or more psychologists review the study’s methods, data collection, and interpretation  
        ~ helps identify biases
        
    
    1. **Researcher triangulation** 
        
        ~ more than one researcher conducting an interview or observation  
        ~ reduce individual bias in the analysis  
        ~ if researchers disagree, they discuss and reach a consensus, leading to more credible findings.
        
    
    1. **Personal reflexivity**
        
        ~ researcher reflects how their personal values, beliefs, and experiences can influence their ability to be objective when carrying out and interpreting the data in their study
        
    
    1. **Epistemological reflexivity** 
        
        ~ researcher reflects on how the research's methodology may have influenced the findings
        
    
    1. **Audit trail** 
        
        ~ thorough documentation of research process  
        ~ allows others to track study, review the process, and check for bias

# Responsibility

### Topics relevant to the concept of responsibility

**Ethical standards** **in research**: use of deception, informed consent, cost-benefit analysis, anonymity, debriefing, right to withdraw, protection from harm

**Interaction with different samples**: Use of animals; child participants; case studies of participants with mental illness, physical disability, or trauma; marginalized groups; consent by proxy.

**Research in public spaces:** research that does not require informed consent.

**Socially sensitive issues**: stigma, publication of findings, advocacy for public welfare.
