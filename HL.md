> [!yellow] # Motivation
- a continuum/spectrum, not separate poles
- the impetus that gives purpose/direction to behvr
- operates in humans at a conscious/unconscious lvls
- person’s willingness to …
### Key components:
**Direction**
- what goal/behvr a person chooses to pursue
**Intensity**
- how much effort a person put into that direction
**Persistence**
- how long a person maintains effort, regardless of obstacles/distractions
### Theories - motivation:
**Evolutionary theories of motivation**
- suggests human behvr is driven by biological instincts evolved to enhance survival + reproduction
- many motivations (e.g. hunger, security, social bonds, sexual desires) are adaptive behvr to increase passing of genes
**Drive reduction theory (Hull)**
- emphasis on motivation from biological needs
- create uncomfortable internal states (aka “drives”)
- behvr seeks to restore balance/homeostasis
e.g. when hungry, find + eat food to satisfy hunger
**Optimal arousal theory**
- motivation to maintain balanced lvl of arousal + void stress/boredom
- neither too high nor too low
- if too low, seek stimulation
- if too high (stressed/anxious), seek reducing tension
**Maslow’s hierarchy (hierarchy of needs)**
- not scientifically backed up
- depends on culture/religion
- a pyramid shape
    - Physiological needs - food (base)
    - Safety needs - shelter/security
    - Belonging needs - love. belongingness
    - Esteem needs - achievements
    - Self actualization - arts? (top)
Instinct = complex nstursl unlearned behvr

### **Intrinsic motivation (umbrella term)**
- engages in activity for own enjoyment, interest, personal satisfaction, feeling of belonging
**Amotivation**
- no motivation at all
==**External regulation**==
- motivation from external rewards/punishments
**Introjected regulation**
- motivation from internal pressures
e.g. guilt, shame, need for approval

**Integration**

## Dweck’s mindset theory
- Carol Dweck
- argues way ppl think abt intelligence affects how they learn + stay motivated
==**Measuring Mindset**==
- often by short questionaries abt beliefs, inteligence, abilites
- often ordinal (scale) questions
- self-reported questionnaires
- widely used tools- Implicit Theories of Intelligence Scale
    - dev by Carol Dweck
    - asks individuals to rate agreement w/ statements
- Behavioural + performance-based measures - observation
    - observe reaction to challenges, failure, effort in learning tasks
    - analyze speech + writing patterns for effort-focused lang vs ability-focused lang (I need to try harder vs I’m just not good at this)
- Neuroscientific methods - fMRI + EEG
    - ppl w/ growth mindset show more brain activity in anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) when making mistakes which suggest more engagement in error processing + learning
    - ppl w/ growth mindset show more activity in problem solving regions (prefrontal cortex) when facing challenges
    - ppl w/ fixed mindset show more activity in emotional regulation areas when facing challenges
### **Fixed Mindset**
- fosters extrinsic motivation
- believes abilities are set from birth
- avoid challenges, gives up easily, sees effort as sign they aren’t smart
- “I know best” “This is good enough” “I give up”, “I’ll never be that smart”, “I want to avoid making mistakes”
### **Growth Mindset**
- fosters intrinsic motivation
- believes practice, hard work, learning from mistakes can improve abilities
- more likely to keep trying, bounce back from setbacks, stay motivated
- “Mistakes help me learn”, “I improve w/ practice”, “Feedback is valuable” “Is this my best work?” “I won’t give up”
### Evaluating Mindset Theory
==**Strengths**==
- simple, clear, easy to apply to real-life settings
- highlights how beliefs abt ability can directly shape motivation + behvr
- helpful for ppl to encourage persistence + resilience
- some research shows shifts towards growth mindset = better effort, resilience, achievement
- empwrs individuals by showing abilities rnt fixed allowing them to take ctrl of learning + growth
==**Weaknesses**==
- Oversimplified - reduces conples processes unto 2 categories
    - motivation is infl by many factors (e.g. emotion, social envir, self-esteem, cultural expectations, personal goals)
    - doesnt fully explain why ppl act the way they do
